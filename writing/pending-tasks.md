## Write an Email: Photography club (7 min)
You are a member of your university's photography club. Last week, the club leader, Daniel Ortiz, announced that the club's monthly photo walk will move from Saturday mornings to Sunday evenings, starting next month. This change creates a problem for you.

Write an email to Daniel. In your email, do the following:
- Explain why the new schedule is a problem for you.
- Describe what you have enjoyed about the photo walks so far.
- Suggest a solution that could work for you and the other members.

*You have 7 minutes. Write as much as you can, in complete sentences, without a spellchecker.*

## Academic Discussion: Free public transportation (10 min, at least 100 words)
Your professor is teaching a class on urban studies. Write a post responding to the professor's question. In your response, you should express and support your opinion and make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

**Dr. Hassan:** Over the past decade, a number of cities around the world have experimented with making public transportation completely free for everyone. Supporters say this reduces traffic and pollution and helps low-income families. Critics argue that the money would be better spent on improving the service itself, for example by adding new routes or making buses and trains run more often. What do you think? Should cities make public transportation free, or should they focus on improving the quality of the service instead? Why?

**Emma:** I think making public transportation free is the best way to get people out of their cars. Many people drive simply because they don't want to pay for a ticket every single day. If buses and trains were free, more people would use them, and that would mean less traffic and cleaner air in the city.

**Kenji:** I disagree with Emma. In my experience, price isn't the main reason people avoid public transportation. Where I live, buses come only every thirty minutes and are often late. Even if they were free, I would still drive. I believe cities should invest in more frequent and reliable service first.

## Academic Discussion: Sugar tax (10 min, at least 100 words)
Your professor is teaching a class on public health. Write a post responding to the professor's question. In your response, you should express and support your opinion and make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

**Dr. Lindqvist:** In recent years, a number of countries have introduced a special tax on sugary drinks such as soda and energy drinks. The goal is to reduce obesity and related diseases by making these products more expensive. However, critics argue that such taxes hit low-income families the hardest and that education about healthy eating would be more effective. What is your view? Should governments tax sugary drinks, or are there better ways to encourage healthier choices? Why?

**Aisha:** I support the tax. Price has a big influence on what people buy, especially teenagers, who rarely think about their long-term health. If a can of soda costs noticeably more, many people will switch to water or unsweetened drinks. The money from the tax could also be used to fund health programs.

**Marco:** I don't think a tax is the right approach. People who really want soda will simply pay more, and it's usually families with less money who feel the difference. Instead, schools should teach children about nutrition from an early age, so they make healthier choices because they understand why, not because they are forced to.
