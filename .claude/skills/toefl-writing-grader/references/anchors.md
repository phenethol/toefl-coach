# Calibration anchors

Original samples written for this skill (not ETS material). The scores are calibration judgments made with the ETS rubric; they exist to pin down what 5, 4 and 3 look like, especially the 5/4 line, where most practice grading goes wrong. Read the annotations, not just the numbers.

> **These anchors illustrate the strict reading** (the coach's *strict estimate*), not ETS's own line. ETS's rated samples in the Official Guide (2026) score a 5 even with plainer language and a few non-impeding errors, more like the "4" anchors below. For the rubric score (ETS prediction), use `ets-calibration.md`.

## Write an Email

**Prompt.** You are a member of your university's hiking club. Last Saturday the club went on a trip with a bus company the club had booked. The bus arrived two hours late, and several members missed a guided tour they had already paid for.

Write an email to Ms. Carter, the manager of the bus company. In your email:
- describe what happened
- explain how the delay affected the club members
- ask for a specific form of compensation

Write as much as you can and in complete sentences.

### Email, score 5 (188 words)

> Dear Ms. Carter,
>
> I am writing on behalf of the Westbrook University Hiking Club about the trip we booked with your company for last Saturday, October 3. The bus was scheduled to pick us up at 7:00 a.m., but it did not arrive untill shortly after 9:00, and the driver could not tell us what had caused the delay.
>
> Unfortunately, the late start had real consequences for our members. Twelve of them had paid $25 each for a guided tour of Pine Ridge that began at 10:30, and by the time we reached the trailhead, the group had already set off without us. Several students were understandably frustrated, since they had planned their whole weekend around this tour.
>
> Given the circumstances, I would like to request a partial refund of our booking fee to cover the tour fees our members lost, or alternatively, a free trip for the club later this semester. I would be grateful if you could let me know which option your company prefers by the end of next week.
>
> Thank you for your time and understanding.
>
> Sincerely,
> Daniel Novak
> Secretary, Westbrook University Hiking Club

Why 5: every bullet point is developed with specifics (date, times, number of members, amount, the concrete consequence). The request is specific, offers an alternative and a deadline, and is phrased politely, which is exactly the "formulation of actions" the rubric names. Register is consistently formal and courteous. Syntax varies for effect (past perfect for the earlier event, a participial opener, a polite conditional request), and the collocations are precise: on behalf of, had real consequences, set off without us, planned their whole weekend around. The one error, "untill", is a typo: a competent-writer slip that doesn't lower a 5. Length is not what makes it a 5: the 4 below loses on precision, specificity and errors, not on word count.

### Email, score 4 (130 words)

> Dear Ms. Carter,
>
> I am writing to you about the trip that our university hiking club made with your company last Saturday. The bus was supposed to come at 7 a.m., but it arrived around two hours later, and the driver didn't give us any explanation.
>
> This delay caused a big problem for our members. Many of them paid for a guided tour in the national park, and because we arrived late, they missed it. They were very disappointed, because they were looking forward to this tour for weeks.
>
> I think it would be fair if your company returns us part of the money we paid for the bus. We would also accept a discount for our next trip. I hope you can answer us soon.
>
> Best regards,
> Daniel Novak

Why 4: all three points are covered and the email is easy to follow on first reading; register is mostly right. Why not 5: elaboration is adequate rather than specific ("many of them", no amounts, no names); the phrasing is correct but plain; and there are several genuine errors: "made a trip" (took), "were looking forward ... for weeks" (had been looking forward), "it would be fair if your company returns us part of the money" (refunded part of the money, or returned part of the money to us), "answer us" (reply to us, get back to us). The closing line is slightly abrupt. Why not 3: nothing makes the reader pause, and every point is developed at least adequately.

### Email, score 3 (95 words)

> Dear Ms. Carter,
>
> I am member of hiking club in my university. Last weekend we had a trip with your bus, but the bus came very late, two hours. It was very bad for us.
>
> Because of this, many members could not go to the tour. They paid money for this tour and they was very angry. Also we were tired because we waited long time in the morning.
>
> I want that you give us the money back. I think this is fair because it was your fault. Please answer me soon.
>
> Best regards,
> Daniel

Why 3: the task is generally accomplished, since all three points appear, but elaboration is thin and generic ("It was very bad for us" adds nothing). Errors are noticeable throughout: articles ("I am member of hiking club", "waited long time"), agreement ("they was"), structure ("I want that you give us"), collocation ("go to the tour", "came very late, two hours"). The range is narrow: short simple sentences joined by because, but, also. Social conventions lapse: the request reads as a demand ("I want that you...", "it was your fault", "Please answer me soon"), too blunt for a complaint to a manager. Why not 2: the meaning is never lost.

## Write for an Academic Discussion

**Prompt.**

*Dr. Okafor:* This week we are discussing how cities pay for public services. Some cities have made public transportation completely free for residents and cover the cost through taxes instead of ticket sales. Supporters say free transit reduces traffic and helps people with lower incomes; critics argue the money could be used more effectively elsewhere. What do you think: should cities make public transportation free? Why or why not?

*Maya:* I think free public transportation is a great idea. If buses and trains are free, more people will leave their cars at home, which means less traffic and cleaner air. It's also fairer, because people with low incomes spend a bigger share of their money on getting to work.

*Tom:* I'm not convinced. Running a transit system is expensive, and if tickets are free, the city has to cut something else or raise taxes. I'd rather see the money spent on making buses more frequent and reliable. People care more about that than about the price.

### WAD, score 5 (146 words)

> Maya and Tom both make fair points, but I think the debate misses a key question: who actually changes their behavior when fares disappear? From what I have read about cities that tried this, many new passengers are people who used to walk or cycle rather than drive, so the drop in traffic Maya expects may be smaller than it seems. That said, I disagree with Tom that the money would simply be wasted. Collecting fares costs money too, through ticket machines, inspectors and payment systems, and removing them speeds up boarding, which partly addresses his concern about reliability. In my view, the most effective policy is a targeted one: free transit for students, seniors and low-income residents, with the rest of the budget invested in more frequent service. That approach would deliver most of the fairness Maya values without sacrificing the quality Tom rightly prioritizes.

Why 5: a clear, qualified position (targeted free transit) that adds ideas neither classmate raised (who actually switches, the cost of collecting fares, boarding speed). It engages both classmates precisely, conceding and rebutting, and explains each claim. Claims are hedged where they should be ("From what I have read"). Syntax varies for effect (colon, relative and participial clauses, the concessive "That said"), and the word choice is precise: make fair points, partly addresses his concern, rightly prioritizes. No genuine errors.

### WAD, score 4 (142 words)

> I agree with Maya that free public transportation can be very helpful, especially for people who don't earn much money. However, I also understand Tom's point about the costs. In my opinion, the best solution is to make public transportation free only for some groups, like students and old people. For example, in my city students get a free monthly ticket, and many of my friends started using the bus instead of asking their parents to drive them. This is good for the environment and it also teaches young people to use public transport from early age. On the other hand, if transportation would be free for everyone, the city would lose a lot of money and maybe the quality of the buses would become worse. So I think a partial solution is better both for the people and for the city.

Why 4: relevant, with a clear position and a real contribution: a concrete example from the writer's city, tied back to the argument. Easy to follow. Why not 5: the cost argument is asserted rather than developed ("maybe the quality of the buses would become worse"); the phrasing is correct but generic (In my opinion, On the other hand, good for the environment); and there are a few genuine errors: "from early age" (from an early age), "if transportation would be free" (were free), "old people" (older people or seniors, a register issue). Why not 3: the contribution is new and supported, and nothing needs rereading.

### WAD, score 3 (106 words)

> In my opinion, public transportation should be free. Like Maya said, it is good for the environment because people will not use cars so much. Also it is helping poor people who don't have money for the tickets. I don't agree with Tom, because the city have a lot of money and they can pay for this. In my country the buses are very expensive and many people complain about it. If it is free, more people will use it and the city will be more clean. Also students can save money for other things. So I think free transport is good idea for every city.

Why 3: relevant, with a clear position, but it mostly restates Maya's two points. The reply to Tom is an unsupported assertion ("the city have a lot of money"), and the example about expensive buses is never connected to the argument, so part of the elaboration is missing or unclear. Noticeable errors: "it is helping" (helps), "the city have" (has), "more clean" (cleaner), "good idea" (a good idea). Narrow range: short simple sentences, the same linkers repeated. Understandable throughout, so not a 2.

## Build a Sentence

All-or-nothing: one chunk out of place, or the distractor used, scores 0.

**Item 1.** Context: "Did you finish the reading for today's seminar?"
Response: "No, ___ ___ ___ ___ it was due today."
Chunks: realize / I / that / didn't / knew
Key: "No, I didn't realize that it was due today." Unused: knew.
Pattern: do-support takes the bare infinitive (didn't realize), so the distractor is a wrong verb form.

**Item 2.** Context: "The library is closing early all this week."
Response: "Do you ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ open on Saturday?"
Chunks: know / whether / it / will / be / does
Key: "Do you know whether it will be open on Saturday?" Unused: does.
Pattern: an embedded question keeps statement word order (whether it will be), so there is no inversion; "does" is bait for learners who invert. "Do you know whether will it be open..." scores 0.
